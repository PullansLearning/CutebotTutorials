1🔥 Warm-Up

Last chapter the remote sent one of three numbers. Three commands, nothing in between. Today the remote sends how far you tilted it, and that is not three values — it is hundreds. The car turns those numbers straight into wheel speeds, so a gentle lean creeps and a hard lean races.

👀 Watch first

📐 Tilt the remote➗ Divide by 10🏷️ Send x and y🔀 Mix into two wheels

📚 Words to know

📐
accelerometer
The part that feels which way the board is tilted.
🔬
milli-g
The unit the tilt comes back in — about -1024 to 1024.
➗
scale
Divide a big range down to fit a smaller one.
🏷️
name and value
Sending a labeled number, so the car knows which is which.
🔀
mix
Combining two numbers to get each wheel speed.

🌍 In the real world

A racing game on a phone steers exactly this way. The phone is not asking which button you pressed — it is asking how far you leaned.

    4🗺️ Plan

    The remote measures and scales; the car mixes. Neither side does the other's job.

    🗺️ Your game plan

    ▸ remote — forever
    ▸ car — a value arrives
    ▸ car — forever
    1. 📐Read both tilts on the remoteForward-back is y, side-to-side is x.
    2. ➗Divide each by 10Turns milli-g into something a wheel can use.
    3. 🏷️Send them with namesSo the car knows which number is which.
    4. 🔀Mix them into two speedsLeft is y plus x; right is y minus x.

    5🔨 Build

    ▶ Open MakeCodeKeep it open beside this page — you will build the blocks there, step by step.

    0 of 5 steps done

    1. Add the Cutebot extension to the car's project: Extensions (just below Math) → "Cutebot".
    2. Build the remote on the spare micro:bit — read, scale, send — then download it and unplug it.
    3. Start the car's program — same group, and two boxes for the numbers as they arrive.
    4. Sort the arriving values by name, then mix them into the two wheels.
    5. Download the car program, unplug, switch on, and pick up the remote.

    🚗 Drive test

    Hold the remote flat and level. The car should sit still. If it creeps, your remote is not as level as you think — that is the accelerometer being honest, not a bug.

    💡

    The mix is what makes it steer. Both wheels take y, so tilting forward drives straight. Only x is added on one side and taken off the other — that difference is the turn.
