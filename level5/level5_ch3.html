1🔥 Warm-Up

You have driven this car with its own buttons, with a second micro:bit, and with a tilt. Today the car listens for a small infrared remote. That remote is not in our kit, so you will press the on-screen remote instead. A real remote needs no group numbers and no second program — but it must point the right way.

📚 Words to know

🔦
infrared
Light your eyes cannot see, used to carry the button press.
📡
IR receiver
The part on the car that watches for that light. It is on pin P16.
👁️
line of sight
Infrared needs a clear path — it will not go through a wall.
🛑
stop
The block that cuts both motors at once.

🌍 In the real world

This is the same technology as a television remote, and it has the same weakness — point it away from the car and it usually does nothing.

    🚗 Drive test

    Press each button on the on-screen remote in Try It. Find that button's branch in your program — does it give the same move? Check OK last: it must stop the car. If your school has an IR remote, put the car on the floor, send it forward, then stop it from three meters away. Then try the same thing standing behind it — the car often does not answer, and that is infrared, not a broken program.

    💡

    Build the stop branch first, then test it. A car you can stop is much easier to work on than one you cannot.
