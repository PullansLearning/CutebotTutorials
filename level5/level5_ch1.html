📋Check-up

1🔥 Warm-Up

Every chapter so far, the car decided for itself — from a button on its own back, or from a sensor. Today the decision comes from across the room. A second micro:bit becomes a remote control. It has no idea what a Cutebot is; it just puts a number on the air. Your car decides what that number means.

👀 Watch first

📻 Same group🅰️ Press A on the remote📤 Send 1📥 Car hears 1↩️ Turn left

📚 Words to know

📻
radio
The micro:bit's built-in way of talking to another micro:bit.
🔢
group
A channel number. Only boards on the same group hear each other.
📤
send
Put a number on the air for anyone in the group.
📥
receive
Blocks that run when a number arrives.
🗝️
code
Here, the number you agree means a particular command.

🌍 In the real world

A television remote does the same thing — it sends a code, and the television decides what that code means. The remote itself knows nothing.

    5🔨 Build

    ▶ Open MakeCodeKeep it open beside this page — you will build the blocks there, step by step.

    0 of 5 steps done

    1. Add the Cutebot extension to the car's project: Extensions (just below Math) → "Cutebot".
    2. Build the remote on the spare micro:bit — three buttons, three numbers.
    3. Start the car's program — same group, and a face so you know it is running.
    4. Add the receiver, and turn each number into a move.
    5. Download the car program, unplug, switch on, and drive it from across the room.

    🚗 Drive test

    Walk to the far side of the room and press A. If it works from there, the radio is doing its job. Then have another pair press their remote — if your car twitches, you are sharing a group.

    💡

    Download the remote program to the spare micro:bit first, and unplug it. Then plug in the car's micro:bit — otherwise you will send the car program to the remote.
