📋Check-up

1🔥 Warm-Up

Everything so far, your car did because you told it to — a button, a timer, even a dice roll. Today it reads the room and decides for itself. The micro:bit screen is also a light sensor: those 25 red lights can measure how bright the room is. Read that number, compare it with one you choose, and the car turns its own headlights on in the dark.

👀 Watch first

📚 Words to know

👁️
light sensor
The micro:bit screen doubles as one. It measures how bright the room is.
☀️
light level
How bright it is, from 0 (dark) to 255 (bright).
🚧
threshold
The number where your program changes its mind.
🔀
if else
Do one thing when the test is true, another when it is false.
🎨
RGB
Red, green and blue mixed to make any color.

🌍 In the real world

A car with automatic headlights does exactly this. So does a street lamp — it is measuring the sky and deciding.

    4🗺️ Plan

    Drive first, then keep asking one question over and over. The answer decides the lights, not you.

    🗺️ Your game plan

    1. 🚗Start driving on startFull speed, before the loop begins.
    2. ☀️Read the light levelEvery time round the loop.
    3. 🚧Compare it with your numberBelow it means dark.
    4. 💡Lights on, or lights offOne branch each — never both.

    5🔨 Build

    ▶ Open MakeCodeKeep it open beside this page — you will build the blocks there, step by step.

    0 of 5 steps done

    1. Add the Cutebot extension: Extensions (just below Math) → search "Cutebot".
    2. In on start, set the car driving at full speed.
    3. In a forever loop, test the light level against your number.
    4. Still inside the forever loop, fill the two branches — white when dark, off when not.
    5. Download, unplug, switch on, and drive it towards a shadow.

    🚗 Drive test

    Hold the car and cup your hand over the micro:bit screen. The lamps should come on within a moment. If they never do, 10 is too low for your room — that is not a bug, it is a measurement.

    💡

    Setting a headlight to 0, 0, 0 is off — no red, no green, no blue means no light. (There is also a Turn off all LED headlights block; today we use 0, 0, 0.)
